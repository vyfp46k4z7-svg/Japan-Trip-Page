Arrive in Tokyo — one night

Tokyo

Check in, dinner nearby, early night. Tokyo itself comes last.

  • Land, clear immigration, and head straight to the hotel — no sightseeing today.
  • Filling in the free Visit Japan Web forms before departure speeds up immigration and customs on arrival.
  • Sort the essentials at the airport: a Suica or Pasmo transit card (or the phone version), cash from an ATM, and a data eSIM or pocket Wi-Fi.
  • Airport trains and limousine buses take about 30 minutes from Haneda and 60–90 minutes from Narita.
  • Dinner near the hotel and an early night — staying up to local bedtime beats napping for jet lag.
  • Have tomorrow's highway bus to Kawaguchiko booked in advance; seats are reserved and the morning buses fill up.
Tokyo→KawaguchikoHighway bus · about 2 hours

Kawaguchiko

Lake Kawaguchi

Mt. Fuji over Lake Kawaguchi

Fuji over the lake from the north shore, best at sunset.

Details ›Lake Kawaguchiko guide ↗
  • Free — the viewpoints are public shoreline along the lake's north shore.
  • About 30–60 minutes for a shoreline stroll, longer if you stop at the lakeside cafes.
  • Fuji is clearest in the early morning; by afternoon it's hidden behind cloud more often than not.
  • A free online Fuji visibility forecast, checked the night before, helps you plan around the clouds.
  • Pairs naturally with Oishi Park, a short walk or bus ride along the same shore.
  • Climate: the Five Lakes sit around 850 m up, so summer days run near 24°C (75°F) — several degrees cooler than Tokyo — with mornings down near 16°C (61°F); pack a light layer for the shore.

Honcho Street, Fujiyoshida

Retro street with Fuji filling the end of the road.

Details ›Honcho Street guide ↗
  • Free — it's a working shopping street in the town of Fujiyoshida.
  • About 15–30 minutes, plus time in the retro shops and cafes.
  • Go early for the clearest Fuji and the emptiest street.
  • The famous photo spot is on a live roadway — crossing staff manage it at busy times; follow their directions and stay on the sidewalks between shots.
  • The surrounding Shimoyoshida neighborhood is good for an unhurried wander.
  • Climate: Fujiyoshida shares the Five Lakes' cooler summer — highs around 24°C (75°F), mornings near 16°C (61°F) — and the open street has little shade at midday.

Oishi Park

Flower fields on the north shore, facing Fuji. Free.

Details ›Oishi Park — Kawaguchiko.net ↗
  • Free entry, with free parking on site.
  • About 30–60 minutes among the flower beds.
  • Lavender peaks from late June to mid-July; autumn brings red kochia bushes instead.
  • The lakeside cafe serves lavender soft-serve and local produce.
  • One of the easiest Fuji photo spots — the flowers, the lake, and the mountain line up in a single frame.
  • Climate: summer highs near 25°C (77°F) with a lake breeze; the late-June to mid-July lavender peak overlaps the tail of the rainy season, so carry a compact umbrella.
  • Highway bus from Tokyo (Shinjuku or Tokyo Station) to Kawaguchiko Station, about 2 hours; reserve seats ahead — roughly ¥2,200 ($14) one way.
  • A deliberate half rest day: the afternoon is left unscheduled on purpose after the flight.
  • Base for two nights on Lake Kawaguchi — the north shore has the classic Fuji-over-the-lake views.
  • Mt. Fuji hides behind cloud most afternoons; mornings and sunsets are the best viewing windows.
  • Local sightseeing buses loop the lake sights, and a multi-day pass pays off across Days 3–4.

Around Mt. Fuji

Fuji Five Lakes

Oshino Hakkai

Eight clear spring ponds fed by Fuji snowmelt.

Details ›Oshino Hakkai — Oshino Village ↗
  • Free to wander the village; the one roofed pond museum costs about ¥300 ($1.90).
  • About 1–1.5 hours, including the food stalls and side lanes.
  • The ponds are Fuji snowmelt, filtered through lava over decades — the water is famously clear and cold.
  • You can fill a bottle at the spring outlets; locals swear by it.
  • About 20 minutes by bus from Kawaguchiko Station; the village soba and tofu stalls make it an easy lunch stop.
  • Climate: summer days around 24°C (75°F), and the spring ponds hold near 13°C (55°F) all year — the air right over the water feels noticeably cooler.

Narusawa Ice Cave

Lava tube near freezing year-round, with blue-lit ice pillars.

Details ›Narusawa Ice Cave ↗
  • Entry about ¥350 ($2.20); a combined ticket also covers the nearby Fugaku Wind Cave.
  • About 30–45 minutes on a short loop route.
  • It stays near 3°C (37°F) all year — bring a jacket even in August.
  • Helmets are provided at the entrance; ceilings are low in places, so watch your head.
  • Formed by a Mt. Fuji eruption over 1,100 years ago; the ice pillars are tallest in spring.

Saiko Iyashi-no-Sato Nenba

Rebuilt thatched-roof village by Lake Saiko — crafts and museums.

Details ›Iyashi-no-Sato — Yamanashi guide ↗
  • Entry about ¥500 ($3.15).
  • About 1–2 hours among roughly 20 restored thatched-roof houses.
  • The village was rebuilt as an open-air museum after a landslide destroyed the original settlement in 1966.
  • The houses hold craft workshops, small museums, and galleries; some activities cost a little extra.
  • Kimono and samurai armor rental is available for photos against the Fuji backdrop.
  • Climate: an open-air village with little indoor shelter — summer highs near 25°C (77°F), cool evenings, and fast-changing mountain weather; bring both sun and rain cover.

Fuji-Q Highland

Record-breaking coasters with Fuji as the backdrop.

Details ›Fuji-Q Highland ↗
  • Entry to the park itself is free — you pay per ride, or buy a one-day pass for about ¥6,000–6,900 ($38–44).
  • Half a day minimum; a full day for coaster fans.
  • Its headline coasters have held world records for speed, height, and steepness.
  • Ride queues are longest at opening for the big coasters — head straight to one.
  • Some rides close in wind or rain, so a clear Fuji day is also the best Fuji-Q day.
  • Climate: fully outdoors at the foot of Fuji — summer highs around 25°C (77°F); rides pause for lightning and heavy rain, both common on summer afternoons.
  • A full day in the Fuji Five Lakes — no hotel change, no packing.
  • The sights are spread around two lakes; local loop buses run every 15–30 minutes and a day pass covers them.
  • Oshino Hakkai and the Aokigahara caves sit in different directions — pick an order and work around the area rather than crisscrossing it.
  • Fuji-Q Highland is the high-energy swap-in if the quiet sights fill the morning.
  • Keep the evening free and get an early night — the climb starts tomorrow.

Summit — then Kyoto

Mt. Fuji · Kyoto

The Summit Climb

Hut overnight, summit at sunrise. Jul–early Sep only.

Details ›Mt. Fuji climbing — official ↗
  • Climbing fee ¥4,000 ($25) per person, paid online in advance or at the trailhead.
  • Mountain huts run about ¥9,000–14,000 ($57–88) with two meals — reserve as soon as bookings open.
  • Two days on the mountain: 5–7 hours up to the hut, a pre-dawn push to the summit, then 3–4 hours down.
  • The season runs early July to early September only; outside it, the trails and huts are closed.
  • Altitude sickness is the main risk — climb slowly, hydrate, and rest at the hut before the summit push.
  • Weather turns fast at 3,776 m — waterproof layers, warm clothing, and a headlamp are not optional.
  • Climate: a different world from the base — even in mid-summer the summit sits between about −5°C and 5°C (23–41°F) with strong wind chill, while the 5th Station starts around 10–15°C (50–59°F).
Mt. Fuji→KyotoBus to Mishima · then Shinkansen

JR Central — Shinkansen ↗

  • Climbing season only: early July to early September — outside it, the summit trails are closed.
  • The standard pattern: climb from the Subaru Line 5th Station in the afternoon, sleep in a mountain hut, and summit for sunrise.
  • A climbing fee (¥4,000 ($25)) and a hut reservation are both required; the entry gates close overnight to anyone without a hut booking.
  • After descending, it's a bus to Mishima Station and then the Shinkansen to Kyoto — expect to arrive in the evening.
  • Luggage forwarding can send the big bags straight from Kawaguchiko to the Kyoto hotel, so only a daypack goes up the mountain.
  • Not climbing? Stay the extra night by the lake and take the same bus-and-train route to Kyoto the following morning.

Kyoto

Kyoto

Fushimi Inari Shrine

Thousands of vermillion torii gates. Free — go at dawn.

Details ›Fushimi Inari — official ↗
  • Free, and open 24 hours — dawn and evening visits dodge the crowds entirely.
  • About 45 minutes for the lower gates; 2–3 hours for the full mountain loop.
  • Roughly 10,000 vermillion torii gates line the trails, most of them donated by businesses.
  • Fox statues guard the shrine — the fox is the messenger of Inari, god of rice and prosperity.
  • Street-food stalls cluster at the base, and the trail gets quieter the higher you climb.
  • Climate: Kyoto summers are famously hot and humid — highs of 33–35°C (91–95°F); the gate tunnels are shaded, but carry water for the climb.

Higashiyama & Kiyomizu-dera

Preserved lanes up to Kiyomizu-dera's wooden stage.

Details ›Kiyomizu-dera — official ↗
  • Kiyomizu-dera entry is about ¥500 ($3.15); the lanes themselves are free.
  • About 2–3 hours, including the walk up through the Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka lanes.
  • The temple's great wooden stage was built without a single nail.
  • Arrive before 9:00 — the preserved lanes are packed by late morning.
  • At the base, the three streams of Otowa Falls are said to grant health, success in studies, or love — tradition says pick just one.
  • Climate: expect 33–35°C (91–95°F) and high humidity; the preserved stone lanes offer little shade — one more reason for the pre-9:00 start.

Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion

Gold-leaf pavilion reflected in the Mirror Pond.

Details ›Kinkaku-ji — official ↗
  • Entry about ¥500 ($3.15).
  • About 1 hour on a fixed one-way garden route.
  • The top two floors are covered in gold leaf, reflected in the Mirror Pond.
  • The pavilion is viewed from outside only — there is no entry inside.
  • Morning light gives the famous reflection; it pairs well with Ryoan-ji's rock garden, a short ride away.
  • Climate: the garden route is entirely outdoors — hot and humid in summer at around 33°C (91°F); mornings are cooler, and the pond reflection is calmest then.

Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

Towering bamboo on Kyoto's western edge.

Details ›Arashiyama Bamboo Grove — Travel Japan ↗
  • Free — it's a public path.
  • About 30–45 minutes for the grove itself.
  • The grove is only about 400 m long — pair it with Tenryu-ji Temple at its end (garden entry about ¥500 ($3.15)).
  • The sound of the bamboo is on Japan's official list of 100 soundscapes to preserve.
  • Before 9:00 it's nearly silent; by midday it's shoulder to shoulder.
  • Climate: the bamboo shades the path, but Kyoto's summer heat — around 33°C (91°F) with high humidity — still applies; the riverside catches a slight breeze.

Gion Evening Stroll

Lantern-lit machiya lanes around Hanamikoji at dusk.

Details ›Gion guide ↗
  • Free — it's a neighborhood walk.
  • About 1–2 hours, with dusk as the sweet spot.
  • Geiko and maiko pass through on their way to evening appointments, roughly 17:30–18:30.
  • Photography is prohibited on Gion's private side streets — signs are posted and fines apply; the main street, Hanamikoji, is fine.
  • Yasaka Shrine, at the end of Shijo Avenue, is free and stays lit into the night.
  • Climate: summer evenings stay warm, around 26°C (79°F) at dusk, so light clothing is enough; sudden evening showers are common in July.
  • A full day with no travel — Kyoto rewards an early start, as most temples close around 17:00.
  • Fushimi Inari first, at dawn: it's open 24 hours, and the gate tunnels empty out above the first viewpoint.
  • The sights sit in clusters — east side (Kiyomizu-dera, Gion) and northwest (Kinkaku-ji, Arashiyama). Taxis between clusters save real time.
  • End in Gion at dusk, when the lanterns come on.
  • One day is a highlights pass; Arashiyama alone can fill half a day if the pace feels rushed.
Kyoto→Nara→OsakaLocal trains · about 40 minutes each

Nara → Osaka

Nara · Osaka

Nara Park & the Deer

1,000+ free-roaming deer; Todai-ji nearby.

Details ›Nara Park — Visit Nara ↗
  • The park is free; a pack of deer crackers (shika senbei) costs about ¥200 ($1.25).
  • About 1.5–3 hours, including Todai-ji.
  • Todai-ji's Great Buddha Hall (about ¥800 ($5.05)) holds one of the largest bronze Buddhas in the world.
  • The deer have learned to bow for crackers — feed one cracker at a time and keep the rest hidden, or they'll follow you.
  • About 40 minutes by train from Kyoto; the park starts a short walk from Kintetsu-Nara Station.
  • Climate: Nara matches Kyoto's summer heat — around 33°C (91°F) and humid; the park's great trees give real shade, but the plaza at Todai-ji is open sun.

Osaka Castle

Keep and museum above massive stone walls.

Details ›Osaka Castle — official ↗
  • Tower museum entry about ¥1,200 ($7.60); the park around it is free.
  • About 1–2 hours, including the museum floors and the rooftop view.
  • The keep is a museum, not the original castle — the 1931 reconstruction covers Toyotomi Hideyoshi and the castle's sieges.
  • The stone walls and moat are original-scale, and the real highlight for many visitors.
  • It closes at 17:00 — on this route it's an afternoon stop, before Dotonbori's evening.
  • Climate: Osaka summer highs run 33–34°C (91–93°F) with high humidity; the park and moat walks are exposed, so the air-conditioned museum doubles as a cool-down.

Shitennoji Temple

Founded 593 — one of Japan's oldest temples.

Details ›Shitennoji — official ↗
  • The grounds are free; the pagoda precinct, garden, and treasure hall each charge a few hundred yen.
  • About 45–60 minutes.
  • Founded in 593 by Prince Shotoku — among the oldest temples in Japan.
  • A flea and antiques market fills the grounds on the 21st and 22nd of each month.
  • The five-story pagoda can be climbed as part of the inner precinct.
  • Climate: the grounds are mostly open — expect Osaka's summer heat, around 33°C (91°F); mornings are the comfortable window.

Dotonbori

Neon, the Glico sign, and street food along the canal.

Details ›Dotonbori — Osaka Info ↗
  • Free to wander; budget about ¥1,000–3,000 ($6.30–19) for grazing on street food.
  • About 1–2 hours in the evening, when the neon is on.
  • Takoyaki (octopus balls) and kushikatsu are the two must-tries — both are Osaka specialties.
  • The Glico running man is best photographed from Ebisubashi Bridge.
  • A 20-minute canal cruise (about ¥1,500 ($9.50)) gives you the district from water level.
  • Climate: a night spot by design — summer evenings sit near 27°C (81°F), warm and humid, with a light breeze off the canal.

Shinsekai & Tsutenkaku

Retro downtown under Tsutenkaku Tower.

Details ›Tsutenkaku — official ↗
  • Tsutenkaku Tower entry is about ¥1,200 ($7.60); the streets below are free.
  • About 1–2 hours, best in the evening.
  • Kushikatsu rule: dip once — double-dipping in the shared sauce is a genuine local taboo.
  • Rub the feet of Billiken, the tower's mascot statue, for luck.
  • The district was built in 1912 with Paris and New York as its models — the retro glow is the point.
  • Climate: best after dark in summer, when Osaka cools toward 27°C (81°F); Tsutenkaku's observation deck is open-air and catches the breeze.
  • Morning train from Kyoto to Nara, about 40 minutes; leave bags in station lockers or forward them to the Osaka hotel.
  • Nara Park and Todai-ji in the morning, while the deer are hungry and the crowds are thin.
  • Afternoon train from Nara to Osaka, about 40 minutes — then Osaka Castle before it closes at 17:00.
  • The evening belongs to Osaka's south side: Dotonbori's neon and street food, then retro Shinsekai under Tsutenkaku Tower.
  • Sleep in Osaka — tomorrow is a full park day with no hotel change.

Universal Studios Japan

Osaka

Universal Studios Japan

Super Nintendo World, Mario Kart, and the Wizarding World. Book ahead.

Details ›Universal Studios Japan ↗
  • One-day passes run about ¥8,600–10,900 ($54–69) with date-based pricing; Express Passes cost extra.
  • A full day — arrive well before opening, as the gates often open earlier than the posted time.
  • Super Nintendo World entry is timed: grab a free area ticket in the app the moment you enter, or guarantee entry with an Express Pass.
  • Express Passes sell out weeks ahead for peak dates — they're the difference between 20-minute and 2-hour lines on headline rides.
  • Single-rider lines quietly cut the waits on several big rides.
  • Climate: a full day outdoors in Osaka's summer — around 33°C (91°F) and humid, with sudden afternoon downpours; a hat, water, and a poncho pay off.
  • A full day at Universal Studios Japan — no sightseeing, no hotel change.
  • Tickets use dynamic pricing and popular dates sell out; book as soon as the date opens, about two months ahead.
  • Download the official app before the day — Super Nintendo World's timed entry and wait times live there.
  • The base stays in Osaka, a short train ride from the park, so the morning start is easy.
  • An early night after the park helps — tomorrow starts with an early Shinkansen to Tokyo.

DisneySea

Tokyo Bay

Tokyo DisneySea

Ocean-themed Disney park found only in Japan. Book ahead.

Details ›Tokyo DisneySea ↗
  • One-day passports run about ¥7,900–10,900 ($50–69) with date-based pricing.
  • A full day; on this route you arrive late morning, so stay until closing.
  • The only DisneySea in the world — and Fantasy Springs, its newest area, is reached through the park app with a free Standby Pass or paid Premier Access.
  • Tickets go on sale one month ahead on the official site and sell out for weekends and holidays — set a reminder.
  • The Tokyo Disney Resort app handles entry, passes, show lotteries, and mobile food orders.
  • Climate: on Tokyo Bay — summer highs near 31°C (88°F) and humid, with a sea breeze that cools things by evening; many plazas are open sun.
  • Start with an early Shinkansen from Osaka to Tokyo, about 2.5 hours — drop bags at the hotel or in station lockers on the way through.
  • Tokyo DisneySea from late morning until closing; the park exists only in Japan, so it earns the travel day.
  • Book the park tickets one month ahead, the day they go on sale.
  • It's the trip's longest day by design — the full rest day tomorrow is placed right after it.
  • Base for the final three nights is Tokyo, so the suitcases stay put from here on.

Rest day

Tokyo

Unscheduled. Rest, or light shopping — it goes straight in the suitcase.

  • A full unscheduled day in Tokyo — the first real day based in the city.
  • Sleep in, do laundry, and let the pace drop after the climb, two theme parks, and three city changes.
  • Optional light shopping only — anything bought goes straight into the suitcase, which is exactly why Tokyo comes last on this route.
  • A buffer by design: if weather disrupted the Fuji days, this is where the plan absorbs it.

Tokyo

Asakusa · Shibuya · Toyosu

Senso-ji Temple, Asakusa

Tokyo's oldest temple, through the Kaminarimon lantern gate.

Details ›Senso-ji — official ↗
  • Free, and the grounds stay open around the clock; the main hall opens early in the morning.
  • About 1–1.5 hours, including the Nakamise shopping street.
  • Tokyo's oldest temple, founded in 628 — the giant Kaminarimon lantern is its symbol.
  • Nakamise's stalls sell snacks and souvenirs; most open around 9:00–10:00, so the temple itself is best first.
  • Fortune slips (omikuji) cost ¥100 (about $0.65) — bad fortunes are tied to the racks on site and left behind, which is the custom.
  • Climate: Tokyo summer highs run 31–33°C (88–91°F) with high humidity; the grounds are open and exposed, so the early start is also the cool one.

Meiji Shrine

Forest shrine beside Harajuku.

Details ›Meiji Shrine — official ↗
  • Free.
  • About 1–1.5 hours, including the forest walk in and out.
  • The forest is artificial — 100,000 trees donated from across Japan when the shrine was built in 1920.
  • Weekend mornings often bring a traditional wedding procession through the courtyard.
  • Write a wish on a wooden ema plaque (about ¥500 ($3.15)) and hang it by the great camphor tree.
  • It sits beside Harajuku — the calmest possible contrast with Takeshita Street, five minutes away.
  • Climate: the forest canopy makes the grounds feel several degrees cooler than the streets — like the high 20s°C (low 80s°F) even when the city tops 33°C (91°F).

Shibuya Crossing & Shibuya Sky

The scramble, then the same view from above. Book Sky ahead.

Details ›Shibuya Sky — official ↗
  • The crossing is free; Shibuya Sky rooftop tickets are about ¥2,200 ($14) booked online.
  • About 1.5–2 hours for the crossing, Hachiko, and the rooftop.
  • Sky tickets go on sale four weeks ahead and sunset slots sell out fast — book early.
  • Up to 3,000 people cross on a single green light at peak times.
  • The Hachiko statue outside the station is Tokyo's most famous meeting point.
  • Climate: the crossing is full-sun pavement on a summer day at 31–33°C (88–91°F); Shibuya Sky's rooftop is open-air and windy, and it closes in severe weather.

teamLab Planets, Toyosu

Barefoot through water and projected flowers. Online tickets only.

Details ›teamLab Planets — official ↗
  • Tickets run about ¥3,800–4,800 ($24–30), priced by date.
  • About 1.5–2 hours inside.
  • Online tickets only, with timed entry — it regularly sells out days ahead.
  • You walk barefoot, including through knee-deep water — wear trousers you can roll up.
  • Several rooms have mirrored floors, so dresses and skirts need care.
  • Free lockers and towels are provided, and phone cameras are welcome.
  • The big explore day — start in Asakusa at Senso-ji before 9:00, ahead of the crowds.
  • Midday at Meiji Shrine and Harajuku: a green, quiet pause in the middle of the city.
  • teamLab Planets runs on timed tickets — book the slot well before the day arrives.
  • Finish in Shibuya: cross the scramble at street level, then watch it from Shibuya Sky — sunset slots go first, so book weeks ahead.
  • This is also the main shopping day, deliberately at the end of the trip — purchases go straight into the suitcase at the hotel.
